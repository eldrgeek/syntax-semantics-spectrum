Syntax → Semantics: a spectrum, not a wall

A question posed by Michael Levin: can the old dichotomy — syntax as the shuffling of marks, semantics as what the marks mean — be re-seen as a continuum? This site's answer is yes, and it builds the continuum twice: once through language and logic, once through the machinery of life.

Prepared 2026-08-11 · Format one shared axis, two parallel essays, a working demo at every station

00 One measuring stick for both wings

Take any symbolic process. Systematically rename its symbols — swap every P for ▲, every codon for a synonym — and ask: does the process still do its job?

The more renamings a process survives, the more purely syntactic it is: its correctness never depended on what the symbols were hooked to. The fewer it survives, the more semantic: at the far pole nothing can be swapped, because the token is welded to a particular referent, occasion, or goal. That single number — substitution-tolerance — turns the two boxes into one axis, and everything interesting lives at the intermediate stations.

The two wings end at the same place from opposite directions: meaning is not a substance that form lacks; it is what form does under contract — grammars, type systems, soundness theorems, genetic codes, bioelectric setpoints, and goals are all ways of spending substitution-freedom to buy a grip on the world.
